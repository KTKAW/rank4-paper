Bounded paths for four-vertex mutation-acyclicity
Abstract
We give an explicit finite criterion for mutation-acyclicity of every integral skew-
symmetric four-by-four matrix. The domain includes arbitrary arrow multiplicities, zero
edges, disconnected support, and singular matrices. The central result bounds an entire
acyclicizing mutation path by an input-computable height: the sum of the six absolute
entries and the absolute ordered Markov expressions for all six cyclic orders. The proof
combines proper-order transport and acyclic endpoint bounds with a lexicographically
minimal witness, minimizing maximum height, the number of states at that height, and
then length. An exhaustive analysis of strong, zero, and unit first edges excludes every
excursion above the budget; the unit cases require rerouting complete words and trans-
porting their tails. Finite-state loop erasure then gives a witnessing word of length at
most (2H(B)+1)6 −1. Exact finite search consequently decides the MA/NMA partition
and supplies sound positive and negative certificates. We explain the mathematical proof,
its Lean realization, and the scope of the recorded verification. The exhaustive fallback
establishes termination but can be enormous; no practical efficiency bound or classification
of all mutation equivalence classes follows.

Proper cyclic orders provide a useful bridge. Mutation-acyclic quivers admit totally proper
orders [4, Theorem 1.1], and proper mutation preserves companion congruence and ordered
scalardata[2,Theorem7.1]. Theseresultsboundacyclicendpoints. Themissingstepaddressed
here is a bound on all intermediate matrices of at least one acyclicizing path. Its proof is the
principal contribution of this paper. The finite decision procedure is then a direct consequence.
Earlier four-vertex classifications concern bounded initial multiplicities [1, Theorem 2.9].
Decreasing-mutation recognition is available in classes of 2-complete acyclic quivers [3, Corol-
lary 3.5 and Remark 3.6]. Neither restricted domain supplies the all-input path bound below.
The resulting theorem resolves unrestricted four-vertex MA/NMA decidability; it does not set-
tle mutation equivalence between two arbitrary matrices or classify every mutation-equivalence
class. No claim of mathematical priority is needed.

4 A total decision procedure and its certificates
Corollary 4.1. There is a total Boolean classifier on all integral skew-symmetric 4×4 matrices
whosetrueoutputisequivalenttoMAandwhosefalseoutputisequivalenttoNMA.Bothoutputs
have sound certificates, and every input receives a certificate of its correct kind.

Proof. Compute H(B) and L(B) exactly. Enumerate all words on {0,1,2,3} of length at most
L(B), perform integer mutations, and test each endpoint for acyclicity. The latter test checks
the 24 vertex orders: in a topological order all upper entries are nonnegative. A successful word
certifies MA. If none succeeds, Theorem 3.1 proves the original all-word NMA statement. Every
operation and list is finite.
The implemented Lean algorithm first tries proved obstruction shortcuts and positive words
through depth three. An unresolved prepass invokes the complete word search through L(B);
it produces no final unknown outcome. A positive certificate is a chronological word checked by
mutation and acyclicity. A negative certificate is either an accepted obstruction with its checker
or an exhausted-search constructor. The exhausted verifier reruns the entire finite search. This
certificate is therefore logically sound but need not be small or cheaply verifiable. A time,
memory, or depth cutoff is never an NMA certificate.
There are (4L(B)+1−1)/3 words in the unpruned fallback list. Even moderate input scalars
can make that number prohibitive. A search of the finite height-box graph is another valid
mathematical procedure, with at most (2H(B)+1)6 states, but the recorded Lean fallback enu-
merates words and does not prune their intermediate states to that box. The proof establishes
termination in exact arithmetic, not completion within the resources of a particular machine.
Theearlierresource-limitedPythonclassifierretainsitsMA/NMA/unknowninterfaceandsaved
abstentions; its unknown outcomes are distinct from the total Lean algorithm.
5 Applications and verification scope
The bound includes many inputs for which simpler certificates are preferable. For example, for
positive integers x,y,z,u,v, the chamber
Q = (0,−x,y,−z,−u,−v)
is MA exactly when xy ≤ v. If xy ≤ v, mutation at 0 gives (0,x,−y,−z,−u,xy −v), topo-
logically ordered as (3,0,2,1). Equality removes edge 23. If xy > v, the unique proper wiggle
class of Q is represented by (0,1,3,2) and (0,3,2,1). Proper mutation at 0 transports it to
the singleton order (0,2,1,3), where the path 2 → 3 → 1 violates properness. The initial class
cannot be totally proper, so Q is NMA. This chamber illustrates a short certificate including
the cancellation boundary; it is not needed for the unrestricted coverage proof.

The path theorem resolves decidability of the MA/NMA partition while leaving other ques-
tions intact. In particular, companion congruence is a necessary invariant, and its proposed
completeness for totally proper quivers remains a separate question [2, Problem 15.17]. Nor
does the total fallback prove that a chosen earlier language of recursive regions or obstruction
certificates is complete. The useful separation is between a uniform mathematical stopping
theorem and the design of efficient certificates for particular inputs.
